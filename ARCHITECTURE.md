# Atlas Architecture v1.1

**Status:** Frozen  
**Date:** 2026-06-06  
**Previous version:** Atlas v1.0 (MVP — form archetypes as primary organization)

---

## Two-Root-Problem Architecture

The entire Atlas is built on two independent problems that every composer must solve:

### Root Problem 1: Balance of Predictability and Novelty

**Question:** How to create an emotional flow through dramaturgy?

The composer must decide *when* a listener expects the known and *when* they need the new. Without predictability, form is chaotic. Without novelty, form is boring.

**Concepts that answer this problem:**

| Concept | Composer Decision |
|---|---|
| Repetition | Where and how much to repeat? |
| Variation | How to vary the theme for novelty? |
| Contrast | Where and how much contrast to introduce? |

---

### Root Problem 2: Organization of Musical Time

**Question:** How to structure musical time?

The composer must decide *where* to divide the material into sections and *how* to orient the listener in time. Without this, form has no architecture.

**Concepts that answer this problem:**

| Concept | Composer Decision |
|---|---|
| Segmentation | Where do sections begin and end? |
| Directionality | Where am I in the time arc? |
| Transition | How to move between sections? |
| Form Archetypes | Which standard template to use? |

---

## Two-Layer Architecture

### Layer 1: Atlas Core

**Concepts:** Segmentation, Repetition, Contrast, Directionality

**Scope:** Macro-level form architecture. Section placement, return logic, contrast strategy, time-arc awareness.

**Sufficient for:** A beginner to build a clear, coherent, and direction-driven form.

**Validation:** 10/20 tracks fully covered (90-100%), 6/20 mostly covered (60-89%), 4/20 process-based forms outside scope.

### Layer 2: Atlas Extended

**Concepts:** Transition, Form Archetypes, Tension, Release, Climax, Variation, Density Control

**Scope:** Meso-level and micro-level decisions. Transition craftsmanship, tension management, climax placement, dynamic shaping.

**Purpose:** Extends the Core for composers who have mastered the basics. Also houses concepts that can be *derived* from Core concepts (e.g., Form Archetypes = Repetition + Contrast + Segmentation).

---

## Architecture Rules

1. **Atlas Core is minimal.** Four concepts, no more. Every concept must pass the Elimination Test: "If removed, does the student become noticeably worse at building form?"

2. **Atlas Extended is optional.** These concepts refine, but are not necessary for a first working form.

3. **No concept describes music.** Every concept enables a *decision*.

4. **No third Root Problem.** All Atlas concepts are derivable from the two Root Problems.

---

## What Atlas Is Not

- Not a musicological classification system
- Not a philosophical ontology of music
- Not a replacement for harmony, timbre, or rhythm theory
- Not a curriculum (separate deliverable)

**Atlas is a tool for compositional decision-making.**
