# 12_BAR_BLUES_DEFENSE.md

**Question:** Should 12-Bar Blues be a Macro-Archetype, a Family, or a Form?
**Verdict:** **Form** — child of Family *Blues*, governed by Macro **VI Cyclic/Groove/Ostinato**.
**Method:** composer-decision logic only. Architecture note; no code.

---

## 1. Why NOT a Macro-Archetype

A macro must force a **unique, irreducible** form decision. 12-bar blues forces:

> *"Build a complete form from a fixed 12-bar I–IV–V harmonic cycle, with AAB phrasing locked to
> it."*

Every clause of that decision is **VI Cyclic with three parameters set**:
- *substrate* = harmonic (not rhythmic) → a VI setting,
- *length* = 12 bars → a VI setting,
- *phrasing* = AAB call-and-response → a VI setting.

Delete "12-bar blues" from the ontology and a composer loses **nothing**: they reconstruct it from
VI + {harmonic, 12, AAB}. By the elimination test, it is **derived, not primitive.** Not a macro.

---

## 2. Why NOT a Family

This is the sharper call. A **Family** is a *cultural tradition that contains several sibling
Forms*; its job is to answer **"which tradition am I working in?"** The tradition here is **Blues**,
and it genuinely contains siblings:

```
Family: BLUES
  ├─ Form: 12-bar blues      (I–IV–V, AAB)
  ├─ Form: 8-bar blues       ("Key to the Highway")
  ├─ Form: 16-bar blues
  ├─ Form: jazz / "Bird" blues (substituted changes)
  └─ Form: minor blues
```

"12-Bar Blues" is **one blueprint inside that family**, not the family itself. The composer
decision proves the level:

- **Family-level decision** ("am I writing blues?") is a *stylistic/cultural* choice — it commits
  you to a feel, a scale, a lyric stance, a tradition. "Blues" answers this.
- **Form-level decision** ("which blueprint — 12-bar, 8-bar, or jazz blues?") is a *structural*
  choice made **after** you are already in the blues. 12-bar answers this.

You do not "compose in 12-bar blues" the way you compose "in blues"; you **pick 12-bar as the
structure** and then pick your dialect (Delta, Chicago, Texas, jazz). Different question → different
level.

**The decisive test:** *a Family must contain Forms.* 12-bar blues contains none — its internal
choices (turnaround, quick-change to IV in bar 2, the bar-5 IV, the V→IV→I cadence) are
**parameters of one blueprint (Form-level decisions)**, not sub-forms. An entity with no children
cannot be a Family. Promoting it would also **orphan its true siblings** (8-bar, jazz blues) or
wrongly nest them beneath it.

---

## 3. Why "it's important" does not change the level

12-bar blues is hugely important — but so are **fugue** and **sonata-allegro**, and both are Forms.
Importance is handled by a **flagship display flag**, not by promotion up the tree. 12-bar blues is
the **canonical teaching Form of macro VI** — the clearest single demonstration that *form can be
pure harmony, no theme*. That earns it visual prominence and "start here for VI" status. It does
**not** earn it an altitude it doesn't structurally occupy. Pedagogical prominence is a *display
property*; ontological level is a *structural fact*. Keep them separate.

---

## 4. Where it lands (final)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Level** | Form |
| **Family (home)** | Blues |
| **Macro (structural)** | VI Cyclic / Groove / Ostinato |
| **Dual-tag** | (none required; pure-VI) |
| **Facets** | `harmonic-cycle`, high *Repetition* core-concept score; `improvised: framed` when used for blowing |
| **Flag** | `flagship` / "canonical teaching Form for VI" |

This keeps the altitude discipline that the whole V3 effort exists to enforce: the **principle**
(Cyclic) is the macro, the **tradition** (Blues) is the Family, the **blueprint** (12-bar) is the
Form. Each level answers a different composer question.
